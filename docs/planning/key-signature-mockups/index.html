Key signature mock-ups

Session 47, for review before the design (key-signature-designer-design.md) is approved. Each card is drawn with the Configurator's own staff, notehead and ledger code, and a prototype of the design's mark list. The dashed grey line is the staff's middle (middle C on these staves). Sharps and flats are drawn here in a serif font; the build would use LilyPond's own glyphs.

A line set is a run of staff lines one staff space apart. The default 4-8 staff has two: the bottom set (E and G♯ below middle C) and the top set (E and G♯ above).

1. Where it sits: the scale stack in C and G major

Today's signature (middle), then on the bottom set, the top set, and both sets. On a set, the signature's octave is centred on the set, and the gold stem runs over its own notes rather than the whole staff. On both sets the root they share is drawn in each copy's own stack, the octave of the lower copy and the tonic of the upper: with stacked sevenths that is a double notehead.

2. A tonic midway up the set

An octave is three staff spaces taller than a set of two lines, so the signature reaches past the set on one side or the other. Three ways to choose its octave, on the bottom set: centred on the set (the reach is split, so it may go below); rising, the tonic at or above a staff space below the set's lowest line (the signature starts in the set and extends up); falling, the top note at or below a staff space above the set's highest line (it ends in the set and extends down).

3. Four ways to stack

The operator's four rules for which stack a note joins, each in the middle of the staff with the upper tonic: stacked sevenths (scale degrees 1 3 5 7 left, 2 4 6 8 right; today's); odd semitones left, even right; a new stack to the right at each change of semitone parity, the tonic's stack first (Clairnote's); and a zigzag, the tonic left and the other stack at each change of parity. In these modes the parity changes exactly at the half steps. The gold stem stands between neighbouring stacks wherever it is on; here it is on for stacked sevenths only. Note the overlap: under the three parity rules a stack holds notes a whole step (two semitones) apart, and these noteheads are 2.7 semitones tall, so they run together; such stacks need marks under two semitones tall, as Clairnote's dots are (sections 5 and 6). Stacked sevenths keep a third between neighbours in a stack.

4. Both sets: the shared root

Repeated on both sets, the root between the copies is drawn in each copy's own stack: the lower copy's octave and the upper copy's tonic. With stacked sevenths those are side by side, a double notehead; odd-left-even-right keeps every pitch on one side, so the root is drawn once. Without the upper tonic the root is the upper copy's tonic alone.

5. A new stack at each parity change, with noteheads (Clairnote-like)

On the Clairnote DN gallery staff, whose noteheads are solid or hollow by whole-tone set, with marks at 70% size, no upper tonic, on the bottom set (centred). A change of parity starts a new stack, so the fill pattern reads as Clairnote's 3–4 for major and 2–3–2 for minor.

6. Custom symbols: Clairnote-like dots with tails

Three symbols imported as shapes (a dot; a dot with a tail rising into it for a sharp; one falling into it for a flat), given to the naturals, sharps and flats. Their fill still follows the noteheads. Stacks go left in sharp keys so a tail does not run into the stack before it. At this size the tails are hard to tell apart, as the operator found of Clairnote's.

7. Repeated on both sets: all notes, or the tonic and the half steps

Repeating the whole scale on both sets is busy; the tonic and the half steps alone carry the key in fewer marks. The last card shows the gold stem's colour and thickness changed.

8. Every second stack, and only the tonic and the half steps

On the default staff, whose noteheads are all solid except the sharps. "Every second stack hollow" tells the stacks apart where the noteheads do not.

9. Traditional accidentals, at the pitch they make

In the traditional order (F C G D A E B for sharps, the reverse for flats), each sign at the semitone of the note it makes (F♯'s at F♯), in the octave the place gives the key's scale. At LilyPond's size a sign is about 2.5 staff spaces tall, ten semitones on this staff, so neighbouring signs overlap in height; they stay readable because each has its own column.

10. Another staff: Kite 4-369

Lines at 3, 6 and 9 semitones in each octave, three semitones per staff space: the sets are three lines each, either side of middle C.
