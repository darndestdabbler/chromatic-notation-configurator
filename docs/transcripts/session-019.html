Session 19: Design: a notehead-first editor

Why. On 2026-11-05 the operator found the configuration screen "extremely complicated and daunting" and proposed redesigning it around the noteheads themselves: the user defines noteheads and the Configurator infers the rules, instead of the user writing rules. This session writes the design for the operator's approval. It changes no code; implementation sessions are planned once the design is approved.

  1. Register.
  2. Write docs/planning/notehead-first-editor-design.md, covering:
  • Flow. The staff editor stays as it is. Once the staff exists, the preview shows 13 placeholder noteheads (C to C′) at default positions, with ledger lines from the default proximity rule. The user can replace the proximity rule with their own ledger rules, or otherwise say when ledgers show.
  • Clicking a notehead sets its colour (an earlier colour or a new one), solid or hollow, shape (the imported shapes plus some defaults), size, pitch class, and pitch-axis nudge.
  • Pitch and pitch axis are two independent variables (operator, 2026-11-06): a central tendency and a variance.
  • *Pitch* is one number: which pitch class sits at the middle of the staff (staff.midpoint), snapped to semitones.
  • *Pitch axis* is a nudge per pitch class, from −1 to +1 semitone, with 0 meaning the notehead is centred on its own semitone. The position is pitch class + nudge. This replaces editing the axis map directly. It exports through today's pitch-axis machinery (octave stride 12, fractional positions, axis units per staff space = the staff's semitones per staff space), so the engine needs no change.
  • Nudges may make neighbours share a spot but may never reorder notes; the control stops there. The Twinline fixture fits in ±1 (its largest nudge is −1, C♯ onto C); its compactness becomes a staff-spacing setting. Say what happens to a config whose map needs more than ±1.
  • Assigning a pitch class is two assignments at once (operator, 2026-11-06): pitch class → staff position (does middle C sit at the centre?), and this notehead's current look (shape, colour, hollowness, size) → that pitch class. Two ways to make them:
  • *Clicking a notehead and naming it* ("make this C") makes both: the spot becomes C, and so does the look drawn there. Every other notehead relabels by its vertical position relative to it.
  • *A label strip on the left of the staff* makes the first alone. The user drags the pitch-class labels up or down, snapping to semitones; each look stays with its pitch class and moves with its label.
  • The labels sit one semitone apart, so they stagger in two columns (even pitch classes left, odd right, or the reverse) to stay readable. Staggering also sets apart labels that share a spot under a nudge.
  • Say whether a nudge travels with its pitch class or stays with its spot under each of the two ways.
  • Rule inference. An algorithm turns the per-notehead choices into notehead rules: a period of 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 or 12 semitones, the accidentals, or else explicit pitch classes, for each of colour, hollowness and shape. Show the inferred rules, and say how a rule that no pattern fits is kept.
  • Previews. Each panel gets a preview, a partial staff or the full staff as it needs.
  • What it retires. Which existing panels and modules it replaces, hides behind an advanced view, or keeps, and what happens to configs whose rules the inference cannot reproduce. The config schema and the export stay as they are unless the document argues otherwise.
  • It has to look good (operator, 2026-11-06: today it "looks like somebody's experiment"). A screenshot review of the current app found:
  • a single 7,200 px column of 11 panels of different widths;
  • developer language on the page (the intro's "shared config object" and "change callbacks", "(Module 17)" and "(set 009)" in titles);
  • one validation warning repeated under nearly every panel;
  • repeated "Preview-only" paragraphs;
  • raw config JSON on the page;
  • a preview cut off at 1440 px with no sign of the missing columns;
  • 11 px grey labels on unstyled browser controls. The design sets the layout, the type, design tokens (colour, spacing, radius) and the wording, so the page speaks to a notation designer. Validation speaks once, in one place. The JSON and the advanced settings move out of the way.
  1. Build mock-ups of the main screens: static HTML under docs/planning/notehead-first-mockups/, using the real preview modules where it can. Screenshot them at 1440 and 1280 px wide, judge them against the design, and revise them until they hold up. The operator approves pictures, not a description.
  2. The document ends with a proposed program of implementation sessions and their release versions, for the operator to approve. The program's first release is the site's first deploy (see "No release until the UI is stable"), so it comes once the new UI is stable, not partway through. Every UI session in that program ends with screenshot checks at 1440 and 1280 px, judged against the approved mock-ups.
