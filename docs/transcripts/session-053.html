Session 53: Designing the key-signature editor

Why. A signature is set today in the panel's rows. It is either "the key's notes", one shape per mark centred on its stack, or "sharps or flats", Emmentaler's signs and nothing else. The operator wants an editor for signatures (2026-11-11): the signature shown magnified, its marks noteheads, sharps and flats or imported symbols, each of which can be resized and moved, with rules for stacking and offsets. It starts from the notehead signature, and for accidentals the arrangement is the cycle of fifths.

Clairnote shows the need (operator, 2026-11-11). A mark on a line or a ledger is solid and one in a space hollow; a flat has a diagonal tail up and a sharp a diagonal tail down. So a mark is one to three shapes. Session 50 tried one-sided tails and dropped them for two reasons (docs/sessions/session-050/notes.md). The engine centres a mark's box on its stack, so a one-sided symbol needed invisible balancing geometry, which LilyPond printed as specks. And a steep tail ran into the next dot of a whole-step stack.

  1. Register.
  2. A design only, no code: an amendment to docs/planning/key-signature-designer-design.md.
  • One model for every signature, in place of the two styles. A signature is four choices:
  • which notes get a mark: every note of the scale, or the altered notes only;
  • what each mark is drawn with;
  • how the marks are arranged;
  • their sizes and offsets.

The notehead signature is the default, and the traditional signature is a starting point: altered notes only, Emmentaler's signs, the cycle of fifths, no gold stem. A config with either of today's styles loads and draws as it does now.

  • Symbols. A mark's symbol is a notehead (the notehead's own look or a chosen shape), Emmentaler's sharp or flat, or an imported SVG. It is chosen separately for naturals, flats and sharps, and imports are separate for flats and sharps. A mark is a base plus up to two added symbols, such as a tail, so one tail serves a solid dot and a hollow one. Each symbol has a reference point that sits on its pitch, such as a flat's bowl line, and says whether it takes ledger lines (a notehead does, a sign does not). An imported symbol has both set when it is imported.
  • When a look applies. Natural, flat or sharp; on a line or a ledger, or in a space; every second stack.
  • Arrangement. Today's stacking rules, or the cycle of fifths with one sign to a column. Traditional signatures zigzag (treble sharps F high, C lower, G high, then D, A, E, B) to stay within the staff, where session 51's signs climb, each in the scale's octave above the tonic. The design gives a chromatic staff a zigzag rule, for example each sign within a window around its line set.
  • The editor. The signature magnified on a canvas, starting from the notehead panel's thumbnails, controls and SVG import. A drag moves or resizes a symbol, in semitones. An edit applies to its category, not to one mark in one key: dragging one flat moves every flat, and resizing a sharp sets session 52's size. A key picker, and small previews of other keys, show what an edit does across the 105 keys, where an offset that fits B♭ may collide in G♭.
  • Placing a mark. By its reference point plus an offset, rather than by centring its box, so no hidden geometry is needed.
  • Tails. How a tail clears the next dot of a stack.
  • The rest. The config, which panel rows the editor replaces, the preview, the mark table's format, and the engine's work: imported symbols as signs, placement by reference point and offset, and layered marks.
  1. Mock-ups, made before the design goes for approval: the editor at 1440 and 1280 px, with its key picker and its previews of other keys; the notehead signature, a traditional one with the zigzag, and Clairnote SN and DN with tails, each in sharp and flat keys at concert size. The operator reviews them.
  2. A cross-provider consult before the operator approves the design. The design plans the sessions that build it, and the engine session they need. The operator decides whether they come before the 1.1.0 release.
